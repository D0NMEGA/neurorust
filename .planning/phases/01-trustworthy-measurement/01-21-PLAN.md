<objective>
Wire the two instruments into `nrmeasure run`, and make every published firmware figure carry
the three statements finding 3 requires, written for the instrument that produced it.

Purpose: plan 01-20 built the parser and the manifest fields. This plan makes the harness able to
take the measurement, and makes the report say what the measurement means.

Three statements have to appear wherever a firmware figure is published, and none of them do
anywhere today:

  1. The instrument detects execution gaps. Those are not uniquely identified SMIs; NMI
     accounting and other hardware effects contribute.
  2. Its output counts threshold-exceeding sampling records, not a census of firmware
     invocations, and several gaps can land inside one record.
  3. Wall-clock duration is not per-CPU exposure.

Two of the three change materially for the instruments this plan wires up, and copying the
`hwlatdetect` caveats verbatim onto `rtla hwnoise` would be its own small dishonesty. `-c 6-11`
runs one sampling thread per listed CPU, so statement 3 is different: exposure is per-thread, not
one thread's polling time divided across a set. And `MSR_SMI_COUNT` is an exact count of system
management interrupts on a specific CPU, which addresses statement 1 head on: it is a census,
with no threshold and no timing inference. It also cannot say anything about duration, which is
the caveat that belongs to it.

Say what is true of each instrument. That is the whole of finding 3.

Runs entirely on the macOS dev host, against fake tool binaries and the committed fixtures. No
rig access; plan 01-23 takes the captures.

Output: `--with-hwnoise`, per-CPU SMI counts in every manifest, and a REPORT.md firmware section
that states what its instrument measured.
</objective>

Write `crates/capture/src/smi.rs`:

```rust
//! `MSR_SMI_COUNT` (register 0x34): an exact per-CPU count of system management interrupts
//! serviced on that CPU since boot.
//!
//! Read before and after a run, the difference is how many SMIs reached that CPU during it.
//! No sampling, no threshold, no inference from timing gaps, which is what three
//! `hwlatdetect` arms spent 45 minutes failing to establish. It answers how many and never
//! how long: a count is not a duration and must not be reported as one.
//!
//! Requires root and the `msr` kernel module. A failed read records its reason; a zero would
//! be indistinguishable from a CPU that genuinely took no SMIs, and those are opposite
//! findings.

pub const SMI_COUNT_REGISTER: &str = "0x34";

/// Parses one `rdmsr -p <cpu> 0x34` reading. The tool prints a bare hex value with no 0x
/// prefix by default and a decimal value under -d; accept both and say which was parsed.
pub fn parse_rdmsr_value(text: &str) -> Result<u64, SmiError>;

/// Reads the register on each CPU. Linux-gated, like `interference::snapshot`.
pub fn read_smi_counts(cpus: &[u32]) -> Result<Vec<CpuCounter>, SmiError>;

/// before minus after, per CPU, with a CPU present in one and not the other reported rather
/// than dropped.
pub fn delta(before: &[CpuCounter], after: &[CpuCounter]) -> Vec<CpuCounter>;
```

so the observed deltas are already per run hour and need no scaling.

```sh
python3 - <<'PY'
import glob, json
for pat in ('calibration-clean', 'calibration-contaminated'):
    p = glob.glob(f'measurements/*-{pat}/manifest.json')[0]
    m = json.load(open(p))
    print('===', pat, p)

```

Set each threshold midway between the two arms on a logarithmic scale, which is the right choice
when the arms are expected to differ by orders of magnitude rather than by a few percent. For each
counter:

```
threshold = round(sqrt(clean_delta_per_hour * contaminated_delta_per_hour))
```

with a floor of `max(clean_delta_per_hour * 4, 100)` so that a threshold never lands close enough
to the clean arm to flap on normal variation. If the two arms differ by less than a factor of 4
on a given counter, that counter does not separate the two conditions: set its threshold to
`null`, leave it out of the verdict, and record why in the `note` field. A counter that does not
discriminate is worse than no counter, because it manufactures false verdicts.

The expected shape of the answer, from the 2026-08-28 evidence: CAL is the counter that separates
strongly (roughly 137,000 on a contaminated 10 minute run versus a near-idle clean run), TLB
should follow it, and RES and device IRQs may or may not separate. Do not assume; read the two
manifests.

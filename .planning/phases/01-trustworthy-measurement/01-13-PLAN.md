<objective>
Take the headline scheduling latency measurement under the finished protocol, and publish a
verdict that reports what was measured rather than a decomposition the instruments do not support.

Purpose: PLAT-03 says worst-case scheduling latency on isolated cores is either brought under
30 us, or the residual is attributed to a named platform cause and published as a documented
limitation. ROADMAP.md success criterion 3 states the same disjunction. Both outcomes are
acceptable and this plan treats them as equally first-class results.

**What changed, and why this plan was rewritten.** The earlier version of this plan required
subtracting an independently measured firmware floor from the scheduling maximum and reporting
the difference as "the largest the kernel's contribution could be". That is not an upper bound
and not a lower bound either. Take a 40 us scheduling maximum caused entirely by kernel activity,
during which no firmware interruption occurred at all; a separate firmware run observes a 22 us
gap; subtracting gives 18 us while the kernel's actual contribution was the full 40. The deeper
problem is that the two instruments do not measure commensurable quantities: cyclictest measures
wakeup latency on the isolated CPUs, a hardware-gap detector counts sampling records whose
interval contained a gap, on whatever CPUs it was pointed at, under its own load and thermal
state, and firmware stalls do not compose additively with scheduling delay. External audit
finding 1.

`render_plat03_verdict` was corrected in commit ece44b0: `Plat03Input` no longer carries a
required `firmware_floor_us`, the subtraction is gone, and the two figures are printed side by
side attributed to their source runs with an explicit statement that they are not combined. Plan
01-21 generalised the observation type so it names the instrument that produced it. This plan
consumes that shape and does not reintroduce arithmetic between the two.

The second thing that changed: plan 01-23 re-took the firmware screen with `rtla hwnoise`, which
runs one sampling thread per isolated CPU, and recorded an exact `MSR_SMI_COUNT` delta per CPU.
For the first time there is a firmware observation about CPUs 6 to 11 rather than about CPU 0 or
CPU 5. It is still reported beside the scheduling maximum and still never subtracted from it.

Landing on "documented limitation" rather than "under 30 us" is a live and reasonable outcome on
this hardware. It is not a failure of the phase; it is the phase working. The point of Phase 1 is
that the number is trustworthy and interpretable, not that it is flattering.

The capture task runs on the Dell Precision 3591. An executor agent working from the macOS dev
host cannot perform it.

Output: one headline run directory and one published verdict document.

  # PLAT-03: worst-case scheduling latency on isolated cores

  ## Verdict
  The first line of this section is one of exactly two, so it is greppable:

      Verdict: under the 30 us gate
      Verdict: documented limitation

  Then the observed figure and the population it comes from:

      Observed maximum: <n> us against the 30 us gate
      Samples at or above 30 us: <n> of <total> (<pct>%)

  The boundary is `>=`. A sample of exactly 30 us is at the gate, not under it, and
  `Percentiles::samples_at_or_above` is the function that counts them. The renderer's own gate
  comparison is the matching strict `<`; commit ece44b0 corrected it from `<=`, which reported a
  maximum of exactly 30 us as under the 30 us gate. Both boundaries agree and the document says so
  in one sentence, so a future reader does not have to check.

  ## What was measured
  The run directory, the duration, the CPU placement (`--cpus 6-11 --main-cpus 0,1`), the
  workload (cyclictest alone, SCHED_FIFO 99, 200 us interval, 400 us histogram bound), and the
  actual operating conditions from the manifest: governor, no_turbo, C-state configuration,
  package temperature at start and end, AC and battery state, and the systemd target. Then any
  protocol deviations, or the sentence that there were none, which is a claim the 15 passing
  precondition results support.

  ## Distribution
  The full percentile table from the generated REPORT.md: p50, p95, p99, p99.9, p99.99 and the
  maximum. Then the counts: total samples, binned samples, overflow samples, and the six
  per-thread maxima listed individually. Per-thread maxima matter here because six similar maxima
  and one outlier mean different things, and a pooled figure hides which happened.
  State the overflow convention: overflow samples are recorded at the histogram bound as a lower
  bound on their true value, so percentiles at or above the overflow fraction are conservative.
  Link the raw capture and state that every figure here is recomputable from it, which
  `nrmeasure verify --strict` now checks mechanically (plan 01-19).

  ## Jitter
  p99 minus p50, one line. This is the metric the project publishes as its headline in later
  phases (BENCH-02, target under 500 us), so recording it here gives the series a Phase 1 origin
  point. Do not editorialise; the comparison against the target belongs to Phase 6.

  ## The independent firmware observation
  Not a decomposition. Report the firmware screen from plan 01-23 as its own measurement: the
  instrument (`rtla hwnoise`), its run directory, the CPUs it observed, its maximum with the
  population that maximum is over, its per-CPU exposure, the operating conditions it was taken
  under, and the `MSR_SMI_COUNT` delta per CPU.

  Then one short paragraph, in the document's own words, saying why it is not subtracted from the
  scheduling maximum: the two instruments measure different quantities, on different CPUs, at
  different times, under different loads, and firmware stalls do not compose additively with
  scheduling delay. A scheduling maximum caused entirely by kernel activity minus an unrelated
  hardware-gap observation is neither an upper nor a lower bound on the kernel's contribution.
  Do not soften this into "we chose not to combine them"; state that combining them would be
  invalid.

  There must be no arithmetic anywhere in this document that takes a figure from one instrument
  and a figure from the other. Not a difference, not a ratio, not a percentage of one expressed
  in terms of the other.

  ## Attribution
  Required when the verdict is `documented limitation`, and worth including either way.

  ROADMAP.md success criterion 3 permits the gate to be missed provided the residual is attributed
  to a named platform cause. Attribute only what a trace supports:

    - If `docs/rig/plat01-stall-investigation.md` records `Outcome: named`, name the kernel path
      and cite the capture that names it.
    - If it records `Outcome: not-reproduced`, say that the earlier stall did not recur across the
      clean hours accumulated, give that exposure in hours, and then state the limit of that
      finding explicitly: non-reproduction adds exposure without an observed recurrence. It does
      not resolve the earlier cause, and it does not establish a bound on how often the event can
      occur. Do not write that the phenomenon is therefore "not part of this residual"; that is
      the inference finding 9 rejects.
    - If it records `Outcome: open-question`, say plainly that part of the residual is
      unattributed, state how large that part is as a number, and link the open question.

  Every event or excursion in this run that no trace explains is called `unattributed`, in that
  word. An honest "this much is unattributed" is worth more than a plausible guess, and the whole
  phase exists to make that distinction visible.

  ## What this figure does and does not support
  Three or four lines. It supports: a maximum observed for wakeup latency on isolated cores on
  this named rig, under this named protocol, over this duration. It does not support: a claim
  about any other machine, a claim about longer durations than were measured, or a claim that the
  worst case is bounded below the observed maximum. Use the phrase "maximum observed under the
  stated test conditions". Do not use "worst-case bound"; a finite screen establishes no bound,
  and that phrasing has already been corrected once in this repository.

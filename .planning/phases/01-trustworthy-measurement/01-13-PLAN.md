<objective>
Take the headline scheduling latency measurement under the finished protocol, and publish the
verdict in the two-number form D-22 requires.

Purpose: PLAT-03 says worst-case scheduling latency on isolated cores is either brought under
30 us, or the residual is attributed to a named platform cause and published as a documented
limitation. ROADMAP.md success criterion 3 states the same disjunction. Both outcomes are
acceptable and the plan treats them as equally first-class results.

D-22 exists because a single number is uninterpretable here. hwlatdetect measured a firmware
floor of 22 us on P-cores under adversarial thermal load and 29 us whole-machine, against a
30 us gate. That leaves roughly 1 to 8 us for everything the kernel does. A reader given only
"the maximum was 34 us" cannot tell whether the kernel consumed 5 us or 12 us of a budget it
never had. So the verdict reports the total against the gate and, separately, the kernel
contribution above the independently measured floor from plan 01-11.

Landing on "documented limitation" rather than "under 30 us" is a live and reasonable outcome on
this hardware. It is not a failure of the phase; it is the phase working. The whole point of
Phase 1 is that the number is trustworthy and interpretable, not that it is flattering.

The capture task runs on the Dell Precision 3591. An executor agent working from the macOS dev
host cannot perform it.

Output: one headline run directory and one published verdict document.
</objective>

  # PLAT-03: worst-case scheduling latency on isolated cores

  ## Verdict
  The first line of this section is one of exactly two, so it is greppable:

      Verdict: under the 30 us gate
      Verdict: documented limitation

  Then the two numbers D-22 requires, in this order and clearly separated:

      Total observed maximum: <n> us against the 30 us gate
      Kernel contribution above the <floor> us firmware floor: <n> us

  State the second number's derivation explicitly: it is the total observed maximum minus the
  firmware floor measured independently on this kernel in run
  `<firmware_floor_source_run_id>`. Say that the subtraction is a bound rather than an
  attribution: the firmware floor and the scheduling maximum were measured in separate runs, so
  the arithmetic gives the largest the kernel's contribution could be, not a measured
  decomposition of a single event. That distinction matters and omitting it would overclaim.

  ## Why one number is not enough
  Two short paragraphs. The gate is 30 us. hwlatdetect measured a firmware floor of 22 us on
  P-cores under adversarial thermal load and 29 us whole-machine, and the RT re-run in plan 01-11
  confirmed or revised those figures (cite the RT numbers, not the stock ones). That leaves
  roughly 1 to 8 us for everything the kernel does. A reader given only a total cannot tell
  whether the kernel consumed most of its budget or almost none of it, so an undecomposed number
  makes a miss uninterpretable and a pass unimpressive. This is why D-22 requires both.

  ## The measurement
  The run directory, the duration, the sample count, the overflow count, and the full percentile
  table from the generated REPORT.md: p50, p95, p99, p99.9, p99.99, max. State the overflow
  convention, that overflow samples are recorded at the histogram bound as a lower bound and that
  percentiles at or above the overflow fraction are therefore conservative. State the
  contamination verdict and that all 14 preconditions passed. Link the raw capture and state that
  every figure here is recomputable from it.

  ## Jitter
  p99 minus p50, one line. This is the metric the project publishes as its headline in later
  phases (BENCH-02, target under 500 us), so recording it here gives the series a Phase 1 origin
  point. Do not editorialise; the comparison against the target belongs to Phase 6.

  ## Attribution of the residual
  Required when the verdict is `documented limitation`, and worth including either way.

  ROADMAP.md success criterion 3 permits the gate to be missed provided the residual is
  attributed to a named platform cause. Name it, using what is actually known:
    - the firmware floor, quantified from the D-18 RT re-run, with its run directory
    - whatever PLAT-01 named, if `docs/rig/plat01-stall-investigation.md` records
      `Outcome: named`, with the named kernel path and its capture
    - if PLAT-01 recorded `Outcome: not-reproduced`, say that the multi-millisecond stall did not
      reproduce under the protocol and is therefore not part of this residual, and bound it by
      the clean hours accumulated
    - if PLAT-01 recorded `Outcome: open-question`, say plainly that part of the residual is
      unattributed, state how large that part is, and link the open question
  Do not attribute a residual to a cause that no capture supports. An honest "this much is
  unattributed" is worth more than a plausible guess, and the whole phase exists to make that
  distinction visible.

  ## What this figure does and does not support
  Three or four lines. It supports: a worst case for wakeup latency on isolated cores on this
  named rig, under this named protocol, over this duration. It does not support: a claim about
  any other machine, a claim about longer durations than were measured, or a claim that the
  worst case is bounded below the observed maximum. State the duration bound explicitly, because
  worst case cannot be established from a single run and a four hour run is a smaller sample than
  the OSADL practice this protocol is modelled on.

  ## Losing configurations
  Per BENCH-06, link `measurements/INDEX.md` and note which runs are excluded from the series and
  why: the reconstructed 2026-08-28 screen (contaminated), the deliberately contaminated
  calibration arm, and every investigation run. State that none was deleted.
